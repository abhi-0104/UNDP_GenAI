INPUT PAPER FOR THE FOURTH INTERNATIONAL CONFERENCE ON FINANCING FOR DEVELOPMENT: THE ROLE OF LANDCENT AND INDIGENOUS KNOWLEDGE IN ADDRESSING POVERTY-RELATED DISEASES AND FINANCING FOR DEVELOPMENT Executive Summary This paper outlines Landcent's strategic approach to addressing poverty-related diseases by integrating cutting-edge health technologies with Indigenous knowledge systems, a resource that has been historically underutilized despite its proven efficacy. This combination will enable the development of affordable, culturally relevant, and highly effective healthcare solutions, particularly suited for marginalized communities where poverty-related diseases are prevalent. By leveraging advanced technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) and involving target populations in co-creation, co-ownership, and benefit-sharing of intellectual property, Landcent aims to foster both product relevance and community acceptance. Landcent's innovative approach aligns with the goals of the Fourth International Conference on Financing for Development (FfD 4), making a compelling case for developmental and philanthropic funding to support the co-development of healthcare solutions that merge traditional knowledge with modern technology. This strategy promises not only to deliver highly relevant, cost-effective solutions but also to promote community empowerment, ensuring long-term health improvements in low-resource settings. I. Introduction Poverty-related diseases continue to pose significant health and socio-economic challenges in many parts of the world, particularly in low-income communities. Addressing these diseases requires both innovative health solutions and a financing framework that promotes inclusivity, scalability, and sustainability. Landcent, an innovative social business organization, is dedicated to the development of affordable health products that leverage advanced technologies and Indigenous knowledge systems. This unique strategy taps into centuries-old practices proven to be effective in health promotion, while also utilizing cutting-edge technologies like AI and ML to accelerate product development. Domestic Public Resources To effectively combat poverty-related diseases, national governments must increase budgetary support for health, particularly preventive measures and low-cost treatments. Public financing strategies should prioritize partnerships with organizations like Landcent that are committed to developing affordable, scalable health interventions. Governments should also promote Indigenous healthcare systems by supporting community-based health initiatives that integrate traditional practices. Additionally, tax incentives for private companies and NGOs that invest in health innovations, including research into traditional medicine, can drive more investment into affordable healthcare solutions. Training healthcare workers to integrate Indigenous health practices with modern medicine will enhance the effectiveness of public health interventions. b. Landcent's work in developing cost-effective healthcare products can be expanded through partnerships with global health funds, philanthropic organizations, and development banks. By incentivizing private sector engagement in public-private partnerships (PPPs) and offering blended financing models, the global community can mitigate risks and increase the impact of health innovations. Equitable financing models should support the documentation, preservation, and commercialization of Indigenous medicinal practices. Communities should benefit financially through shared intellectual property rights, ensuring that traditional knowledge is integrated into healthcare systems without compromising cultural integrity. c. Financing from debt relief should support both modern innovations and Indigenous health initiatives, scaling up the availability of affordable healthcare products. f. III. Emerging Issues The increasing impact of climate change on health outcomes demands innovative solutions. Climate change exacerbates the spread of poverty-related diseases, making it essential to incorporate climate resilience into healthcare strategies. Landcent’s vector control, medicinal, and diagnostic tools are designed for adaptability in climate- affected regions, while Indigenous knowledge provides sustainable approaches to managing natural resources and health. The global financing framework must consider the intersection of climate change, poverty, and health, supporting cross-sectoral interventions that address these challenges simultaneously. IV. Data, Monitoring, and Follow-Up Effective financing requires robust data systems that track progress in health outcomes and financing commitments. The framework should include indicators for assessing the impact of integrating modern and Indigenous health solutions on poverty-related diseases. Monitoring mechanisms should be designed to capture financial inputs and qualitative improvements in health service delivery and community empowerment. Investing in participatory data collection approaches, where communities document the effectiveness of health interventions based on traditional practices, can provide valuable insights for scaling successful models. Additionally, reporting frameworks should account for the economic benefits of reduced healthcare costs due to using low-cost, innovative health technologies. V.
